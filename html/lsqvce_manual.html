1 Overview

lsqvce is a MATLAB toolbox for least-squares adjustment with variance component estimation, built for satellite gravity recovery (GRACE / GRACE-FO) and equally at home on a levelling network. Version 5 (September 2026) is the result of the R5 review track: one accumulator, one stochastic-model estimator, one loop with three policies, one user surface.

1.1 What it does

  • Adjusts a linear or linearised Gauss–Markov model with a common parameter block and per-system, per-day local blocks eliminated on the fly (the block-arrow structure of satellite gravimetry), on one accumulator with a QR or normal-equation backend.
  • Estimates the stochastic model of every observation stream jointly (Ellmer 2018): a spectral shape per pool group, a level per stream, optional per-arc variance factors, by Hutchinson-probed redundancies – Estimate="noise".
  • Regularises by declaration: priors on common and on local parameters are pseudo-observation groups in your columns, counted in the redundancy, with their sigma fixed or estimated by Helmert / Koch–Kusche.
  • Diagnoses: Baarda / Pope data snooping, redundancy numbers, reliability, the whiteness of every stream, a five-level verdict (R.report).
  • Robust M-estimation (IRLS) and damped Gauss–Newton on the same loop.
  • Watches: Options.OnPass fires after every pass with the state and the live accumulator; R.plot afterwards.

1.2 The three sentences you need

lsqvce_setup                                  % once
R = lsqvce.fit(data, Estimate="noise", Pool="obsType");   % the one verb
R.report                                      % the verdict

data is a plain struct (chapter 3); everything about how to run is a lsqvce.Options (chapter 4); everything that comes back is a lsqvce.Results (chapter 5). The theory behind every number is lsqvce_theory.pdf (chapter 11).

1.3 Reading order

2 Installation

2.1 Requirements

MATLAB R2024a or later (developed and gated on R2026a). The Parallel Computing Toolbox is optional (Parallel.Mode="parfor"|"spmd", Parallel.DistributeDays); without it the toolbox runs serially and says so once per run.

2.2 Install

cd <toolbox root>          % the folder holding +lsqvce
lsqvce_setup               % or lsqvce_setup('install')

This adds exactly two path entries (the root with the lsqvce.* packages, and examples/), builds the Help browser index so that doc lsqvce opens this manual, saves the path, and prints a check report. Not added by design: dev/tests (the suites manage their own path), template/ (you copy it into your project), dev/.

                           % Help browser database it built; the folder itself is untouched

3.0.1 0a. The v5 delta (R5 M5): three additions, no shape changes

  1. data.priors(k) = struct('group' | 'L', 'x0', 'sigma', 'from', 'name', 'estimate') — declared pseudo-observation groups L·x ~ x0 with weight 1/sigma² on the COMMON parameters, folded into the solve AND into the diagnostic sweep (they carry a redundancy). A free network’s datum is the prior L = B' on the null space: the exact minimum-constraint solution, for any weight, because the data’s range and the null space are orthogonal (lsqvce:fit:badPrior on malformed fields). A Kaula rule or a background model is the same declaration with a scientific L. With estimate = "scale" (R5 M7 7b) the group has its OWN variance component: under Estimate="noise" its sigma is updated every pass by the Helmert / Koch–Kusche rule (Ω_k = ‖(L x − x0)/σ‖², r_k = p − tr(L N⁻¹ L’)/σ², σ² ← σ²·Ω_k/r_k; the fixed point Ω_k = r_k is “the prior’s weighted residual sum equals its redundancy”), so λ = s0²/σ_k² is the regularisation the data support; R.noise.priors and R.noise.priorHistory record it. from is the pass at which the group becomes active (default 1; a single-pass run reports lsqvce:fit:priorNotActive and skips it). A group is declared EITHER by group — a regular expression on the NAME column of commonParams (data.paramColumns.name, default 2); fit builds L, one row per matched row per instance — OR by L (p × nc in the columns of A); never both (R5-D8 round 6). One sigma per group: the rows of L are the members, and several rows are what make estimate="scale" estimable (a single row has redundancy ≤ 1). 1b. data.systems(k).priors(l) = struct('group' | 'L', 'x0', 'sigma', 'from', 'name', 'estimate') (R5-D8 round 5/6) — the priors on system k’s LOCAL parameters, the literal counterpart of data.priors(j): one struct per GROUP, declared EITHER by group — a regular expression matched against the NAME column of the table (column 2; data.paramColumns.name to say otherwise); the toolbox builds L with one row per matched template row per instance — OR by L (q × nl in the columns of that system’s B as lsqvce.layout(data) announces them, used as is); never both. Then x0, ONE sigma per group (relative weights inside a group go into L), from, name (default the pattern), estimate = "none" | "scale". Every row must lie inside one day’s local block (couple days through common parameters); the width of L is checked by name (lsqvce:fit:badPrior). Each group rides as its own observation stream prior_<name> of the system (pseudo-observations A = 0, B = L/σ, l = x0/σ, unit law), so accumulation, elimination, the sweeps, residuals() and the redundancy count treat it as data — and with estimate = "scale" its σ is estimated every pass of Estimate="noise" as that stream’s level, the Helmert / Koch–Kusche factor the joint estimator computes for every stream: IDENTICAL behaviour to the common groups (R.noise.model{k}.sigma for stream satKey|prior_<name>). Common priors and every system’s priors carry their own settings. THE RULE (R5-D8): the parameters and their columns are yours (tables, buildDay, plots, toTable); regularisation is the toolbox’s (mechanism, redundancy, activation, VCE per group); the interface between them is an operator in your columns, announced by lsqvce.layout, checked by width. Options.Reg / RegLambda / RegMatrix are gone. 1c. data.paramColumns = struct('start', c1, 'end', c2) (R5-D8 round 3) — names the columns of BOTH tables that hold a row’s validity ([MJD seconds] pairs or fractional MJD; [] = always). A 'day' / 'arc' row is then instantiated only on the calendar days its validity overlaps (half-open day: start < d+1 and end > d); 'common' / 'system' rows are always present. nc and nl are derived accordingly, and buildDay’s 6th argument tells you which template rows exist on the day: info.arcRows, info.dayRows, info.sysRows, info.commonDayRows (indices into the tables, in block order). Without paramColumns every row is valid always — the v4 behaviour. This is how a one-day manoeuvre parameter and a “literal” row become the same mechanism as everything else.
  2. Per-stream policy on the covariance spec — covariance.<type>.estimate = "none" | "scale" | "law" (default "law" under Estimate="noise") and .arcFactors (logical, default the Options policy). A law whose sigma is a VECTOR (per-row weights) is a fixed structure: it can carry a level ("scale") but not a spectral shape ("law" is refused, lsqvce:fit:lawOnFixedStructure). Options.Pool decides which streams SHARE one estimated spectral shape; every stream keeps its own level. To read an arc’s standard deviation back, use all three terms: declared sigma × sqrt(arc factor) × R.sigma0 — the level and the factor split one scale by a convention (eq. 6.5.30’s gammaBar) and sigma0 carries the rest, as in any GLS adjustment; measured on a fixture planted at 0.5 / 1.5 / 0.5, that reconstruction gives 0.517 / 1.499 / 0.507 (R6 WP-100). (R6 finding F-01, fixed in WP-98: eq. 6.5.28 multiplies by the factor the weighting used, so the ratio now settles across passes — 9.4 / 8.6 / 8.6 / 8.6 against a planted 9, where before it grew by 8.4x every pass. Runs with arcFactors = false are unchanged.)
  3. The per-pass inspection hook — Options.OnPass = @(ctx) receives, after every pass of every iterative run, the pass state, the live accumulator, ctx.data (this declaration as given) and ctx.processingOptions{k} (system k’s processingOptions as declared); return false to stop. Nothing else about buildDay or the tables changed.

Semantics made explicit in v5: a full SPD weight matrix returned by a batch source is whitened exactly (chol(P)), and per-observation diagnostics of a batch problem are reported in the WHITENED frame (R.diag.v = sqrt(p).*v; only their sums are frame-invariant).

3.0.2 0b. The v5.3 delta (R6 WP-90): the error information travels with the day

What the preprocessing knows about the accuracy of every observation — a per-epoch standard deviation, per-axis standard deviations, or a full 3 × 3 covariance per epoch — is DATA, and it comes back from buildDay WITH the day, as D.Cep (§3.4, §7), for ANY stream: nEpoch × 1 for a scalar stream, nEpoch × d or nEpoch × d × d for a multi-axis one. The axis count is derived from it. The covariance spec (§5) declares the TEMPORAL law only — diag (white), arma, psd, acf — and the toolbox composes C = D (R_t ⊗ I_d) D per arc. Retired with this: the separable type (it was arma + Cep), the epochCovariance hook (an unknown field now), and a static Cep on the spec (refused when the day carries one: one fact, one authority). Nothing else about the declaration changed.

3.0.3 0. The v4 principle: state nothing the data already states — and scope, don’t number

The contract declares structure, never counts, and since v4 it declares scope, never numbering: column 1 of both parameter tables is a scope token, the rows are templates, and the toolbox expands them over the calendar. Every count is derived:

quantity derived from
nc (common unknowns) the expansion of data.commonParams ('common' rows once + 'day' rows per calendar day with arcs)
nl (local unknowns) the expansion of localParams ('arc' rows per (day, arc) + 'day' rows per day with arcs + 'system' rows once); not declared → nl = 0
observation types fieldnames(covariance)
u (batched nonlinear) numel(x0)

The v2 count fields are gone (v3), and so is v3’s hand-numbering split (localsPerArc XOR free localParams): one mechanism, the scoped table. Every column offset reaches buildDay through its 6th argument (info, §6.3) — no offset is ever computed in user code. The driver was the GRACE parameterization: arc-wise ICs, day-wise ACC scale/bias/drift, per-arc KBR empiricals — three scopes, one table.

3.0.4 1. The mental model in one paragraph

You describe one adjustment problem with a plain MATLAB struct called data. It answers three questions: what is estimated for everybody (data.commonParams, one row per common unknown), which days are processed (data.days), and which observing systems deliver data (data.systems, one entry per satellite or instrument). Each system points to one function you write, buildDay, that returns the design matrices and observations of a single day. Everything else — reading days in the right order, cutting them into arcs, whitening with your covariance model, iterating the variance components — is the toolbox’s job:

opts = lsqvce.Options(Estimate="noise");  % what to do
R    = lsqvce.fit(data, opts);           % the one verb
R.report                                 % the verdict

You never call makeLoader yourself; fit builds the loaders from data. R.systems (the existing Results field) records what ran: per system its id, nl, the loader closure, the day list and the name.

3.0.5 2. The data struct — field by field

data is a scalar struct. Field names are case-sensitive and use lowerCamelCase. Unknown field names are an error (lsqvce:fit:unknownField), which is deliberate: a typo in a struct field would otherwise be silently ignored. The retired v2 names (common, numCommon, numLocal, observationTypes, systemId, cache, loader, the plot hooks) are unknown fields now — the error names the field, so a v2 declaration fails loudly, not subtly.

3.0.5.1 2.1 data.commonParams — the shared unknowns (required)

field type / size meaning
commonParams cell, R×C (C ≥ 1) one row per common template. Column 1 is the scope token: 'common' (one column of A, always) or 'day' (one column per calendar day that carries at least one scheduled arc in at least one system). Columns 2…C are your convention (degree, order, epoch, …). nc is derived from the expansion; the table is piped to buildDay as its 3rd argument. 'arc' is forbidden here — arc sets are system-specific (lsqvce:fit:badScope).

Binding column layout of A: the 'common' rows first (table order), then one day block per calendar day (calendar order — the sorted union of every declared day list). buildDay receives this day’s block position as info.commonDayOffset / info.commonDayCount (§6.3). Minimal table when you have nothing to say: [repmat({'common'}, nc, 1), num2cell((1:nc).')].

3.0.5.2 2.2 data.days — the default schedule

field type / size meaning
days double, 1×D day identifiers, e.g. 1:30 or datenum values cast to integers. Every system uses this list unless it carries its own days. Required unless every system has one.

Days must be numeric integers: the loader cache is keyed on the number.

3.0.5.3 2.3 data.systems(k) — one entry per observing system

data.systems is a 1×M struct array (M ≥ 1). All entries have the same fields; fill an unused optional field with [].

Required — two fields

field type / size meaning
buildDay function handle your day builder, §3.
covariance struct one field per observation type holding a covariance spec (§5), e.g. cov.kbr = struct('type','diag','sigma',0.2). The fieldnames ARE the observation types (their order is the processing order); buildDay is called once per (day, type).

Optional

field type / size default meaning
arcs double or fh 1 which arcs each day carries, §4. ('fromDay' is ENGINE-tier only.)
localParams cell, R×C (C ≥ 1) {} the local templates, §6: column 1 is the scope token 'arc' | 'day' | 'system', columns 2…C your convention; nl is derived from the expansion. Passed to buildDay as its 4th argument. Not declared → nl = 0 (§6.4).
days double, 1×D data.days per-system schedule override.
id double 1×1 or single char the index k system identifier, unique across systems (explicit ids and index defaults share one namespace). Longer labels go in name.
name char / string '' human-readable label used in reports.
processingOptions struct struct() anything your buildDay needs (paths, force models); passed as its 5th argument.

Rule of thumb for beginners: buildDay + covariance is a complete system. Add arcs when a day has more than one arc, 'arc' template rows in localParams when arcs carry their own unknowns, the rest when you need it. (The v5.2 epochCovariance hook is gone — the per-epoch error information comes back from buildDay as D.Cep, §7.)

3.0.5.4 2.4 A pitfall you will hit once: struct() with cell values

% WRONG: struct() expands cell values into a struct ARRAY
s = struct('localParams', {'bias','drift'});          % s is 1x2
% RIGHT: double braces, or assign field by field
s = struct('localParams', {{'bias';'drift'}});        % s is 1x1
s.localParams = {'bias'; 'drift'};                    % clearest

3.0.6 3. The buildDay contract

3.0.6.1 3.1 Who calls it, and how often

fit calls buildDay once per (day, observationType) and cuts the returned day into arcs itself. Every pass over the data reads each day twice (once to accumulate the normal equations, once for the residuals), and the variance-component loop multiplies that by the number of passes. Two consequences:

  1. Determinism is mandatory. Same arguments → byte-identical output. Random numbers, clocks, mutable globals are forbidden inside buildDay. (The residuals are formed as v = l − A·x − B·y from the second read; any difference lands in σ₀.)
  2. Cache expensive reads yourself. The toolbox caches one built day; if the raw file read is the expensive part and serves several observation types, keep your own cache in the closure that binds buildDay.

3.0.6.2 3.2 Signature — declare only what you need

fit passes as many of these six arguments as your handle declares (nargin):

D = buildDay(day, obsType)                                        % classic
D = buildDay(day, obsType, common, local, procOpt)                % parameter-driven
D = buildDay(day, obsType, common, local, procOpt, info)          % with the expansion info
argument what it is
day one entry of the day list (numeric)
obsType one entry of fieldnames(covariance) (char)
common data.commonParams
local localParams of this system (or {})
procOpt processingOptions of this system
info this day’s expansion slice — every offset of A and B (§6.3); [] only when nothing is scoped

A varargin builder receives all six.

3.0.6.3 3.3 Return value — required fields

field size meaning
A m×nc design matrix of the common unknowns, all observations of the day
B m×nl design matrix of the local unknowns; may be sparse (recommended when arc-wise)
l m×1 observed minus computed (the reduced observation). Not the raw observation.
t m×1 epoch of each row in seconds (the whitener needs the lags)

Return raw, unwhitened matrices. Whitening happens per arc inside the toolbox from your covariance spec, and the VCE loop reweights there too — a builder that scales its own rows would be reweighted twice.

3.0.6.4 3.4 Return value — optional fields

field size meaning
arcIdx cell, 1×nArc row-index vector of each arc (lengths may differ). Absent → the whole day is one arc.
arcId double, 1×nArc the identity of each arc, when the schedule is ragged (§4.2). Must name every arcIdx entry exactly once with unique positive integers.
Cep nEpoch×1, nEpoch×d or nEpoch×d×d the error information of this day (§7): the per-epoch standard deviation of a scalar stream, the per-axis standard deviations, or the full per-epoch covariance of a multi-axis stream. nEpoch·d must equal the row count; the axis count d is derived from it.
nAxis 1×1 the axis count of a multi-axis stream WITHOUT Cep (rows epoch-major, m = nEpoch·nAxis, §7). Derived from Cep when that is present; given as well, it must agree.

3.0.6.5 3.5 What is checked, and the error you get

error id meaning
lsqvce:makeLoader:buildDay your buildDay threw; the original error is attached as the cause
lsqvce:makeLoader:badDay a required field (A, B, l, t) is missing
lsqvce:makeLoader:dayShape A, B, l, t do not share a row count
lsqvce:accum:arcBadNc / arcBadNl A or B has the wrong number of columns — the derived nc/nl are enforced against every day
lsqvce:makeLoader:badArc a positional arc id is out of range (static schedule declared wrongly), or an arc of a multi-axis day does not hold whole epochs
lsqvce:makeLoader:badArcId arcId does not name every arcIdx entry exactly once

lsqvce:makeLoader:axisConflict nAxis disagrees with Cep (or with a d on the spec), or does not divide the row count
lsqvce:makeLoader:cepTwice the covariance spec carries a Cep AND the day returns one
lsqvce:covModel:cepOnVar a var law (its own cross-axis structure) with a Cep

These are raised while reading, by the engine, and keep their engine ids. Errors in the declaration itself are raised by fit before any data is read and carry lsqvce:fit: ids (unknownField, noSystems, noCommon, missingField, localCount, noDays, badId, duplicateSystem, builderKind, badArcs, and every makeLoader construction id relabelled, e.g. lsqvce:fit:badScope).

3.0.7 4. Arc schedules

3.0.7.1 4.1 Static — every day looks the same

s.arcs = 4;            % arcs 1..4 on every day, every observation type
s.arcs = [1 2 4];      % these ids on every day
s.arcs.kbr = 2;  s.arcs.pos = 4;    % per observation type

Arcs are then addressed by position in arcIdx; an id beyond numel(arcIdx) is an error, which catches a mis-declared schedule.

3.0.7.2 4.2 Ragged — days differ (gaps, manoeuvres, short months)

Declare a resolver that returns the arc ids of a day without reading data:

s.arcs = @(day, obsType) myArcTable{day};      % 1xA vector of ids for that day

and let buildDay return arcId naming the arcs it actually carries. Arcs are then addressed by id; an id that a day does not carry is treated as a data gap and skipped — no padding, no empty arcs.

(The v2 string form arcs = 'fromDay' is not part of this contract: it reads data before the run starts and cannot be combined with localsPerArc. It survives as an ENGINE-tier convenience on lsqvce.engine.makeLoader; fit rejects it with lsqvce:fit:badArcs.)

3.0.8 5. Covariance specs — the covariance.(obsType) structs

One struct per observation type; type selects the temporal law — how the errors of one stream are correlated down the epochs. The most common:

type fields use for
'diag' sigma (scalar std) uncorrelated noise, e.g. struct('type','diag','sigma',0.2)
'arma' ar [1 a1 … ap], ma (default 1), sigma (innovation std) coloured noise with a known filter
'acf' acf @(dt,opt), sigma correlation given as a function of the lag
'psd' f (nf×1, Hz), S (nf×1) noise given as a one-sided PSD
'var' ar (d×d×p), sigma (d×d) vector-AR for multi-axis streams with cross-axis temporal correlation (no Cep)

sigma is always a standard deviation, never a variance, and it is the LEVEL of the law. The accuracy of the individual observations — what varies from epoch to epoch — is not declared here: it comes back from buildDay with the day as D.Cep (§7), and the toolbox composes the two. The VCE loop scales and reshapes these specs at run time; you only give the starting point. Full reference: doc lsqvce.engine.covModel.

3.0.9 6. Local unknowns — the scoped localParams table

One mechanism: the table’s rows are templates, column 1 says how often each row instantiates.

3.0.9.1 6.1 The three scopes

s.localParams = [ ...
    [repmat({'arc'}, 6, 1),  num2cell((1:6).')];   % 6 ICs   -> once per (day, arc)
    {'arc','kbrBias'; 'arc','kbrCos1cpr'};         % 2 more  -> once per (day, arc)
    {'day', 'accScale'; 'day', 'accBias'};         % 2       -> once per day with arcs
    {'system', 'antennaOffset'}];                  % 1       -> once, full adjustment
token one block per … typical use
'arc' (day, arc) of this system initial conditions, per-arc empiricals
'day' day whose schedule carries ≥ 1 arc ACC scale/bias/drift
'system' whole adjustment instrument constants; also the v3 free table (see migration)

Requires days and a schedule resolvable without reading data (static or resolver) whenever 'arc'/'day' rows are present; 'system'-only tables need neither. nl is derived; declaring numLocal too is the conflict error.

Binding column layout of B (per system): per day (declared order) — the arc blocks in sorted arc-id order (each block = the 'arc' rows, table order), then the 'day' block — and after all days one trailing 'system' block. A day whose schedule has no arcs gets no blocks at all (§6.4).

3.0.9.2 6.2 Heterogeneous per-arc counts stay ENGINE-tier

A template has the same rows for every arc. If arcs genuinely carry different counts, that is the ENGINE-tier lsqvce.engine.makeLoader(..., 'LocalsPerArc', @(day, arcId) n) (the WP-52 plan) — deliberately not on the fit surface.

3.0.9.3 6.3 Using info (buildDay’s 6th argument)

info.arcId            % 1xA  ids of this day's arcs
info.offsetOf         % @(arcId) -> column offset of that arc's block in B
info.count            % 1xA  columns of each arc block (= number of 'arc' rows)
info.dayOffset        % 1x1  this day's 'day' block offset ([] if none)
info.dayCount         % 1x1  its width (0 if none)
info.sysOffset        % 1x1  the 'system' block offset
info.sysCount         % 1x1  its width
info.nl               % 1x1  total local count of the adjustment
info.nc               % 1x1  total common count
info.commonCount      % 1x1  width of A's leading 'common' block
info.commonDayOffset  % 1x1  this day's common day-block offset in A ([] if none)
info.commonDayCount   % 1x1  its width (0 if none)

Fill B(rows, info.offsetOf(id) + (1:k)), A(:, info.commonDayOffset + (1:w)) — and never compute an offset yourself.

3.0.9.4 6.4 Days without arcs, systems without locals

A calendar day on which no system schedules an arc gets no common day block (nc shrinks); a system day without arcs gets no arc and no 'day' blocks (nl shrinks) — offsets stay gap-safe on ragged schedules and manoeuvre days. A system without local unknowns is still the default: declare no localParams and return B = zeros(m, 0) (the engine skips the pre-elimination; empty diagnostics per HANDOVER G-07).

3.0.10 7. The error information — D.Cep on the returned day

The covariance of a stream has two parts. The temporal law R_t (white, AR, a PSD) is declared in §5 and estimated by Estimate="noise". The per-epoch accuracy D — what the preprocessing knows about each observation: a PPP covariance, a per-epoch sigma from a fit, a weight from an earlier screening — is DATA, and you return it with the day:

D.Cep = sigmaPerEpoch;                     % nEpoch x 1      scalar stream
D.Cep = [sx sy sz];                        % nEpoch x 3      per-axis sigmas (diagonal D)
D.Cep = permute(reshape(Q.', 3, 3, []), [3 1 2]);   % nEpoch x 3 x 3  full per-epoch covariance

The toolbox composes C = D (R_t ⊗ I_d) D per arc: every epoch is de-scaled by D^-1/2 (for d = 1 that is a division by the sigma), then the temporal whitener runs down the epochs of each axis. The axis count d is size(Cep, 2); nAxis is derived from it. Rows of a multi-axis stream are epoch-major (x₁ y₁ z₁ x₂ y₂ z₂ …), so nEpoch·d must equal the row count, and an arc must hold whole epochs. Under Estimate="noise" the estimator de-scales the residuals of every arc by the same D before it forms the lag statistics, so the estimated law is the law of the normalised errors — a scalar stream with a per-epoch sigma is handled exactly like a 3-axis one.

Rules:

  • One authority: Cep lives on the day. A Cep on the covariance spec together with one on the day is refused (cepTwice); a d on the spec that disagrees with the day is refused (axisConflict).
  • A multi-axis day without Cep (set nAxis) is composed with D = I and reported once per run (lsqvce:makeLoader:noCep) — the omission is distinguishable from a choice.
  • sigma on the spec stays the LEVEL of the law; Cep is relative to it. Declare sigma 1 when Cep already carries the absolute accuracy.
  • A var law carries its own cross-axis structure and takes no Cep.

4 Options — the complete reference

lsqvce.Options is a typed value class: every knob has a type, a range and a default, checked at assignment (MATLAB:validators:* identifiers) or at construction (lsqvce:Options:*). It says how to run; everything about the data, including regularisation, is declared on data (chapter 3). Three equivalent spellings:

opts = lsqvce.Options(Estimate="noise", MaxPasses=6);   R = lsqvce.fit(data, opts);
opts.Noise.Grid = 2160;                                  % change one later (validated)
R = lsqvce.fit(data, Estimate="noise", MaxPasses=6);     % straight on fit

disp(opts) lists every knob, a * marking the defaults. The option space is 14 top-level knobs and 5 groups; the count is pinned by a test so it cannot grow unnoticed.

4.1 What to estimate

Estimate — "none" (default) | "noise". "none" is one generalised least-squares solve under the declared covariance laws. "noise" runs the joint variance-component estimator (Ellmer 2018, theory ch. 6): for every stream whose spec allows it (covariance.<type>.estimate ~= "none"), the spectral shape of its noise is estimated per pool group from Hutchinson-probed redundancies, one level per stream, and (with ArcFactors) one factor per arc; prior groups with estimate="scale" get their sigma by Helmert / Koch–Kusche in the same passes. The v4 words map as: "ellmer" → Estimate="noise"; "vce" → Estimate="noise", Pool="stream", ArcFactors=false; "pooled" → Estimate="noise", Pool="all", ArcFactors=false.

Pool — "all" (default) | "obsType" | "stream". Which streams share one estimated spectral shape: all streams of the run; the streams of one observation type across systems (both satellites’ KBR, say); or every (system, type) stream on its own. Every stream keeps its own level regardless. Fewer groups = more arcs per shape = a steadier estimate; more groups = more physics.

ArcFactors — logical, default true. Per-arc variance factors (theory 6.5.4): every arc of a stream gets a keyed factor on top of the stream’s level, normalised to unit mean over the run. false = one level per stream (the Helmert factor of its arcs). Turn off when arcs are few or short.

Robust — "none" (default) | "huber" | "igg3" | "bisquare". IRLS M-estimation on the same loop, every data kind: the influence function named here reweights the standardised whitened residuals with a MAD scale each pass; the final factors are R.history(end).factor. Not combinable with Estimate="noise" in one run (refused by name: run the robust pass with Estimate="none").

4.2 The loop

MaxPasses — integer ≥ 1, default 10. The one pass budget of every iterative run (variance components, IRLS, Gauss–Newton). Exhausting it returns the last pass with R.stopReason = "maxPasses" and one warning.

MinPasses — integer ≥ 1, default 1. Passes run even if the tolerance is already met (GRAVFIRE’s minimumIterations).

Tolerance — positive, default 1e-2. The stop rule of the variance-component loop: max |α² − 1| over frequencies and arcs below it; for Gauss–Newton the relative step in units of the formal error; IRLS uses RobustOpts.Tol.

Probes — integer ≥ 0, default 100. Hutchinson probes for the per-lag redundancies of the joint estimator; 0 = the exact route (measured to be the better default up to u ≈ 4000; the probe route is faster only for the dense inverse at small nc).

Seed — [] (default) or a double. RNG seed for the probes; recorded in R.meta.seeds so a run is reproducible.

4.3 Run control

Backend — "qr" (default) | "normal". Square-root (Householder QR, rank refusal by rank()’s tolerance) or normal equations (Cholesky). Same numbers to rounding; QR is the safer default on ill-conditioned common blocks.

CacheDays — positive, default 1. Built days kept resident per system. A two-stage pass builds every day twice (accumulate + sweep) and a VCE run 2n+1 times; Inf keeps the calendar and builds each day once per run — your memory decision (a built type-day is m × nc doubles, ≈190 MB at degree 60 for a 6-h three-axis piece).

Verbose — logical or 0..2, default false. Screen messages: 0 silent, 1 progress, 2 detail. (Level 3 was merged into 2 in 2026-07; lsqvce.util.verbosity refuses it, and since v5.3 so does Options itself, by name.)

LogFile — string, default "". Mirror the messages to this file.

OnPass — [] (default) or @(ctx). Your inspection hook, called at the end of every pass of every iterative run with one struct: driver, pass, x, Cxx, sigma0, s0sq; the loop state (noise: C, Cgroups, sigma2, arcKeys, streams, sigma, white, whitePval, maxDev, skips; IRLS: factor, keys, scale, dx; Gauss–Newton: vPv, dxRel, lambda); acc — the live accumulator (call lsqvce.engine.residuals(ctx.acc, …) or any engine function inside the hook); data — your declaration as passed to fit; processingOptions{k} — system k’s, as declared. Plot, drawnow, save wherever you like. Return nothing to continue, false to stop after the pass (R.stopReason = "userStop", the last pass returned with its covariance). A throwing hook is not swallowed: it arrives as lsqvce:callUser:threw with your error as the cause. This replaces the v4 plotPostSolve / plotPostModel hooks and ShowSeconds / ExportDir.

6 Regularisation: priors and groups

6.1 The rule

Parameters and their columns are yours — the tables, buildDay, plots, toTable. Regularisation is the toolbox’s — the mechanism, the redundancy, the activation pass, the estimation of its strength. The interface between them is an operator in your columns, announced by lsqvce.layout, checked by width.

6.2 What a prior is

Tikhonov regularisation of a parameter is an additional observation equation, “parameter j is observed to be x₀ with standard deviation σ”:

L · x = x₀ + v,   weight 1/σ²

where L is the coefficient matrix of the pseudo-observations (one row per pseudo-observation) exactly as A is the coefficient matrix of the observations. The toolbox therefore treats a prior as data: it enters the normal equations, it has a redundancy (how much of it the data overrule), and estimating its σ is variance-component estimation on that group (Koch–Kusche).

6.3 The declaration — one mechanism for common and local parameters

data.priors(j)            = struct(...)    % on the common parameters, L in the columns of A
data.systems(k).priors(l) = struct(...)    % on system k's local parameters, L in the columns of B

One struct per group, with the fields

field meaning
group a regular expression matched against the name column of the table (column 2; data.paramColumns.name to say otherwise). The toolbox builds L: one row per matched template row per instance (per day for a 'day' row, per arc for an 'arc' row, once for 'system'/'common').
L the explicit operator (q × nc or q × nl) in the columns that lsqvce.layout(data) announces. Give group or L, never both.
x0 the a-priori values (q × 1, or a scalar; default 0)
sigma ONE standard deviation for the rows of the group. Relative weights inside a group go into L (divide a row by its own relative σ); the struct’s σ is the common factor.
from the pass at which the group becomes active (default 1). In a single solve a from > 1 group is reported (lsqvce:fit:priorNotActive) and skipped.
name the label (default the pattern); local groups become the stream prior_<name>.
estimate "none" (default; σ fixed) or "scale": σ is estimated every pass of Estimate="noise" by the Helmert rule σ² ← σ²·Ω/r — the fixed point Ω = r (“the prior’s weighted residual sum equals its redundancy”), so λ = s₀²/σ² is the regularisation the data support.

Several rows make σ estimable. A group with one row has redundancy ≤ 1 and cannot estimate its own σ (it is left unchanged and flagged in R.noise.priorHistory). The x/y/z accelerometer scales of both satellites over a month are one group with 180 rows.

Local rows live in one day. A row of a local group has its non-zero columns inside one day’s local block (or the system block); coupling two days is done through the common parameters. Different days of the same parameter are different rows — that is how the estimate pools information across the calendar.

6.4 How they are treated

Common groups are stacked into one prior on the common block, folded into the solve and appended to the diagnostic sweep (they carry a redundancy). Local groups ride as their own observation stream prior_<name> of the system — pseudo-observation arcs with A = 0, B = L/σ, l = x₀/σ and a unit law — so accumulation, elimination, the sweeps, residuals() and the redundancy count see them as data, and under Estimate="noise" the group’s σ is the stream’s level: identical behaviour to the common groups, no second estimator. R.noise.priors, R.noise.priorHistory (common) and R.noise.model{k}.sigma for stream satKey|prior_<name> (local) report the result.

6.5 The datum of a free network

Minimum constraints Bᵀx = 0 are the group L = B', x0 = 0: when B spans the null space the solution is the exact minimum-constraint one for any weight (the data’s range and the null space are orthogonal). Without it the accumulator refuses a rank-deficient system by name (lsqvce:qr:singularR); it never guesses.

7 Stochastic models and their estimation

Every observation stream carries a covariance spec in data.systems(k).covariance.<type> (chapter 3 lists the fields) — its TEMPORAL law: diag (white), arma / ar (a whitening filter), acf / psd (an exact Toeplitz law). The per-epoch accuracy of the observations (a sigma per epoch, or a full d × d covariance per epoch for a multi-axis stream) is not declared: buildDay returns it with the day as D.Cep (chapter 3, §7) and the toolbox composes C = D (R_t ⊗ I_d) D per arc — for every temporal law, scalar streams included. Batches are overlapped by the filter memory automatically, so batching never changes the result.

With Estimate="noise" the joint estimator (theory ch. 6, Ellmer 2018) runs on every stream whose spec allows it (estimate = "law" default; "scale" = level only, the declared shape kept; "none" = untouched). Per pass it accumulates and solves under the current laws, sweeps every arc once for its whitened residual and Hutchinson-probed redundancies at every lag (all lags from one FFT per column), updates the spectral shape per pool group in the PSD domain (positive by construction, resampled to every arc’s length), the level per stream, the per-arc factors, and the prior groups’ σ; it stops at Tolerance or MaxPasses. A law with a per-row sigma is a fixed structure: it can carry a level but not a spectral shape.

What comes back: R.noise.Cgroups (one covariance function per pool group), R.noise.stream / R.noise.model{k} (per stream: the level, its law, its arc factors), R.history.streams / sigma / white / whitePval per pass (the Ljung–Box whiteness of every stream, K = 20, α = 0.05), R.convergence(), R.plot("psd"), R.plot("convergence").

The acceptance of the estimator is physical: a planted field and planted stream sigmas are recovered within their formal errors (demo_gravity, counted in the test suite).

8 Diagnostics and the verdict

Every solve carries per-observation diagnostics in R.diag (residuals in the whitened frame sqrt(p).*v, redundancy numbers, standardised residuals, the Baarda / Pope test with Diagnostics.Alpha and Bonferroni, minimal detectable biases, reliability, the a-priori backstop). R.report(Level=0..4) reduces them to one verdict and its evidence; R.summary() and R.convergence() read the pass record of an iterative run; R.residuals() re-reads the arcs for the whitened residuals per stream; R.contribution() gives the information share of every stream in the common parameters; lsqvce.engine.snoop iterates data snooping with a clean re-solve on the block path; R.compare(Rred) is the nested-model F test between two fits. For correlated batches the per-observation quantities live in the whitened frame; only their sums are frame-invariant.

11 Theory

lsqvce_theory.pdf (the R5 theory, 54 pages) is the authority for every formula the toolbox implements: the block-arrow adjustment and its two backends, the joint variance-component estimator (the per-lag redundancies, the PSD-domain law, the per-stream level and the arc factors), priors and Koch–Kusche, the diagnostics (Baarda, Pope, reliability, whiteness), IRLS and Gauss–Newton, and Appendix C, the option mapping as implemented. The literature: Ellmer (2018), Kvas (2019), Koch and Kusche (2002), Baarda (1968), Pope (1976).
