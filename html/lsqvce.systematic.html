lsqvce.systematic

Detect unmodelled systematic errors in the residuals.

Syntax

S = LSQVCE.SYSTEMATIC(D) runs three complementary tests on the normalised

Description

residuals from lsqvce.diagnose. This closes the single largest blind spot in the rest of the toolbox.

Why This Exists

--------------- A smooth unmodelled signal -- a thermal drift, an unmodelled bias that varies with elevation, a once-per-revolution term you forgot -- produces NO single large residual. Data snooping therefore reports a perfectly clean result while the parameters are quietly corrupted. Meanwhile the global chi-square test rejects, because the residuals ARE too large in aggregate.

That combination -- inflated variance factor, zero outliers -- is the signature of a systematic error, and until now the toolbox could only tell you to go and plot something. Demonstrated on a sinusoid of amplitude 0.8 sigma: s0^2 = 1.33 (inflated), Bonferroni snooping flagged 0 observations, and the three tests below returned p = 5e-5, p < 1e-16 and p = 3e-9.

The Three Tests

--------------- (a) RUNS TEST on sign(w). Distribution-free. Counts sign changes: a drift

produces too few. Weak on smooth signals, strong on steps.

(b) LJUNG-BOX on w, lags 1..h. Detects autocorrelation of any shape.

Powerful, but see the caveat below.

(c) BINNED MEANS of w against each supplied covariate. In bin b the mean

of w has standard deviation 1/sqrt(n_b) under H0, so z_b = mean_b *
sqrt(n_b). Bonferroni over the bins. This is effectively a score test
for an omitted parameter, and it is the WORKHORSE: in testing it
reached 84-100% power where the runs test managed 1-9%.

(a) and (b) require the residuals to be ORDERED along the systematic -- they see the sequence you hand them. (c) does not: it works on any covariate directly. Pass covariates and let (c) do the work.

CAVEAT ON LJUNG-BOX -- and it is an honest one. Least-squares residuals are NOT independent, even when the observation errors are: cov(v) = sigma0^2 * Qvv, and Qvv is not diagonal. Normalising to w removes the variance but not the correlation. Ljung-Box therefore OVER-REJECTS, and the effect grows with u/n. Measured false-alarm rate at a nominal 1%:

u/n = 0.060  ->  1.6%
u/n = 0.012  ->  1.3%
u/n = 0.003  ->  0.6%   (within noise of nominal)

Geodetic problems live at u/n << 0.01, where it is harmless. The toolbox warns when u/n > 0.05, and it leads with the binned test, which is exact.

Name-Value

'covariates'  struct: each field is an n x 1 vector to bin against.
              e.g. struct('time', t, 'elev', e, 'azim', az)
              THIS IS THE IMPORTANT ONE. Supply everything you have.
'nbins'       bins per covariate (default 10)
'lags'        Ljung-Box lags h (default 10)
'alpha'       significance (default 0.01)
'verbose'     print (default true)

Output S

.runs      struct(z, p, nRuns, expected)
.ljungBox  struct(Q, p, h, reliable)
.bins      struct array, one per covariate:
           (name, z, p, worstBin, worstZ, edges, counts, means)
.detected  true if any test rejects at alpha
.worst     name of the most significant finding

Example

% The failure that snooping CANNOT see. A smooth drift produces no
% single large residual: the variance factor inflates, the flag list
% stays clean, and THAT COMBINATION is the signature.
[~, D] = lsqvce.adjust(u, src, 4, 'verbose', false);
t = (1:n).';
S = lsqvce.systematic(D, 'covariates', ...
                       struct('time', t, 'elev', elev));
% S.detected     did anything fire?
% S.ljungBox.p   autocorrelation in the residual sequence
% S.runs.p       distribution-free sign test
% S.bins         one entry per covariate: .name, .p, .worstBin
% The COVARIATE ranking lives in S.bins -- S.worst ranks every test,
% and a drift in time necessarily also trips Ljung-Box.
[~, iw] = min([S.bins.p]);
fprintf('worst covariate: %s\n', S.bins(iw).name);
